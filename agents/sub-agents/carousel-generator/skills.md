# Playbook — carousel craft

## 1. Hook families for slide 1
The headline on slide 1 is the whole ballgame. Reach for a proven family and write the actual line:
- **Number / promise** — *"7 cold-email lines that get replies"* — concrete payoff, open loop.
- **Negative / mistake** — *"Stop ending emails like this"* — loss aversion.
- **Contrarian** — *"Posting daily is killing your reach"* — pattern interrupt.
- **Curiosity gap** — *"The slide 9 trick nobody uses"* — withholds the payoff.
- **Direct callout** — *"Founders who hate selling: read this"* — self-selection.
Pick the family that fits the material. Never label the family on the slide — just write the line.

## 2. The swipe test
After drafting, reread slides 1→N asking only: *"would I swipe?"* Any slide where the answer is "meh" gets rewritten or cut. The most common fix is ending a body on tension ("…but that's not the real problem.") instead of resolution.

## 3. Visual-note grammar
One line per slide, written for an art generator, not a designer's mood board: **subject + treatment + emphasis**. Good: *"big numeral '3' off-center, headline beneath, brand accent color block behind"*. Bad: *"something clean and engaging"*. Keep visual continuity — note the recurring motif once on slide 1 (e.g. *"this carousel: dark base, single accent color, oversized type"*) and only call out deviations after that.

## 4. CTA discipline
One CTA, one action. "Follow for more + comment GUIDE + check link in bio" is three CTAs and converts like zero. Match it to the goal you were given: educate → save/follow; leads → comment-keyword or DM; authority → share/repost.
